About me

      

        
        

          
I'm from Kisoro, Uganda, and trained as a software engineer at Makerere University. That gives me a wide base: I work as a software engineer, an IT engineer, an AI engineer, or something in between. I moved to Norway for a master's in Applied Computer and Information Technology, specialising in Applied AI, at OsloMet.

          
Today I'm head engineer at the University of Oslo, where I lead development of Dilli og de magiske ordene, a language-learning app for children. Before that I built chatbots and research software, and I've published research on detecting plant disease with deep learning. In my free time I share machine learning and deep learning ideas with smart people, for example at KnowTheRisk AI.

Selected work

      
A language-learning app for children, conversational AI, and deep learning research on plant disease.


      

        

          Mobile and web app
          
Dilli og de magiske ordene

          
A research-based language-learning app for kindergarten children with weak language skills, including children with intellectual disability and Down syndrome. Children join Dilli on an adventure and collect new words through play.

          
It is part of the Down Syndrome LanguagePlus (DSL+) project at the University of Oslo.

          

            
Platforms
iOS, Android tablets and web

            
Research
University of Oslo researchers, together with children, kindergarten teachers and special educators

            
My role
Head engineer. I lead the engineering: architecture, implementation, testing, deployment and maintenance, working closely with the researchers and designers.

            
Funded by
The Research Council of Norway and the Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training

            
Releases
Free to use. The first version came out on 15 February 2026. A second version followed in October 2026 with AAC (ASK) support. The next is planned for 15 February 2027, with more releases to follow.

          AI research
          
Potato plant disease detection with hybrid deep learning

          
A first-author paper in BMC Plant Biology (2025), written with Ayan Chatterjee (NILU) and Raju Shrestha (OsloMet). It combines convolutional and transformer models to classify potato plant diseases from images.

          
The paper is open access and the code is public on GitHub.

          Conversational AI
          
Chatbots and AI avatars

          
I built user-facing chatbots at Again X so customers could help themselves, and an educational chatbot at the University of Oslo. At OsloMet I led a seven-person student team that built a multimodal AI avatar.

          
I work with language models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), LangChain, and speech-to-text and text-to-speech, with Python backends in FastAPI and Flask.

What I use it for

          
Claude and Codex help me with drafts, boilerplate, tests, refactoring, documentation and finding my way in unfamiliar code. Routine work goes faster, and I get more time for the hard parts.

          
I treat it like a quick colleague who still needs a review.

        

        

          
          
What stays with me

          
Architecture, design choices, security and the final decision. I need to understand what I ship and why it works.

          
The skills come first. The tool comes second.

        

        

          
          
How I stay in control

          
I read and test everything the tool writes. I keep sensitive data out of prompts, and I check claims against the documentation.

          
A tool can be wrong. I'm the one who answers for the result.
